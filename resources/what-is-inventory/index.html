What Is Inventory?

Inventory is the stock your business owns and hasn't sold yet, valued at what it cost you — the foundation every stock-related decision in your business is built on.

Written by: BizTrack Pro Team · Reviewed by: BizTrack Pro Team · Last updated: August 2026

Direct answer

Inventory is the stock your business owns and hasn't sold yet, valued at what it cost you. "Stock" and "inventory" are generally used interchangeably by shop owners — there's no meaningful difference in everyday use, though "inventory" is the more formal accounting term for the same thing.

Basic stock valuation

Formula
Stock Value = Quantity on Hand × Cost per Unit
Worked example

A shop has 150 padlocks left on the shelf, each costing ₵80.

Quantity150
Cost per unit₵80
Stock Value₵12,000

Why accurate stock records matter

Your stock value feeds directly into Cost of Goods Sold, which feeds into Gross Profit and eventually Net Profit. If your recorded stock doesn't match what's actually on the shelf, every number built on top of it is wrong in the same direction — not just your inventory count, but your entire profit picture.

How this connects to real problems

Once inventory is tracked accurately, three separate questions become answerable: whether stock is disappearing somewhere it shouldn't (Stock Loss), whether stock is present but not selling (Dead Stock), and how fast stock overall is moving through the business (Stock Turnover). If your records themselves are unreliable, none of those three questions can be answered honestly — that's what the stock accuracy diagnostic is for.

How BizTrack Pro handles this: stock deducts automatically the moment a sale is marked paid, so the recorded quantity moves in step with what actually left the shelf, instead of depending on someone remembering to update a count separately.

Common mistakes

Mistake: Valuing stock at selling price instead of cost
Stock value for accounting purposes uses what you paid, not what you plan to sell it for — using selling price overstates the business's actual assets.
Mistake: Updating stock counts only occasionally
A count that's only accurate on the day you did a physical stock-take drifts further from reality every day after — see the stock accuracy diagnostic if this is a recurring problem.
