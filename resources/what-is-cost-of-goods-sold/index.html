What Is Cost of Goods Sold?

Cost of Goods Sold is what it actually cost your business to have the stock available to sell — the foundation every profit and margin number in your business is built on.

Written by: BizTrack Pro Team · Reviewed by: BizTrack Pro Team · Last updated: August 2026

Direct answer

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) is what it actually cost your business to have the stock available to sell — the price you paid your supplier, plus anything directly tied to getting that stock ready to sell (like delivery). It does not include rent, staff wages, or other running costs.

Formula
COGS = Opening Inventory + Purchases − Closing Inventory

"Cost of sales" is the equivalent term used in international accounting standards — see the IFRS Foundation's Glossary for how accounting terms are formally defined.

Why it's calculated this way

You don't need to track the cost of every individual item sold if you know what stock you started the month with, what you bought during the month, and what's left at the end. What's "missing" from that total is what was sold — and its cost.

Worked example

A shop starts the month with ₵12,000 of stock, buys ₵14,000 more during the month, and ends the month with ₵12,000 of stock still on the shelf.

Opening Inventory₵12,000
+ Purchases₵14,000
− Closing Inventory₵12,000
COGS₵14,000

Common mistakes

Mistake: Using total purchases as COGS
If you bought ₵14,000 of stock but only sold ₵10,000 worth of it, your COGS for the month is ₵10,000 — the rest is still sitting in inventory, not yet "cost" you anything from a sales standpoint.
Mistake: Not knowing your closing inventory value
Without an accurate stock count (or a system that tracks it automatically as sales happen), COGS becomes a guess — and every number built on top of it, including gross profit and gross margin, inherits that guess.

Why this matters beyond the formula

COGS is the number every other figure in this cluster depends on. Get it wrong, and your gross profit, gross margin, and eventually your net profit are all wrong in the same direction.
